Nine Messages — The Goblin Rosetta Experiment

Two independently trained small neural cultures, nine paired utterances, and one unusually important architecture assumption.

Open the interactive exhibit ↗

Try it

Compare eight sightings with nine. At eight, 24 slot-matching explanations survive and the translator must abstain. Reveal the ninth to resolve the slot order, then choose new four-feature meanings. Try the unknown glyph and known-glyph swap disturbances: the first causes abstention; the second shows undetected drift.

What the records show

Ten pairs of independently seeded small speaker/listener networks were trained, with 512 sampled meanings per tribe and 4,096 possible feature combinations. In their prescribed compositional four-slot channel family, the translator got 40,960/40,960 held-out and seen meaning translations correct across the ten pairs, including 31,334 combined unseen combinations. Eight contacts left 24 compatible dictionaries; the ninth selected one.

This is a bounded channel-family result, not arbitrary-language decoding.

What failed, and why that matters

A holistic random-codebook control was rejected rather than translated; unfamiliar glyphs trigger abstention. But replacing one familiar symbol with another after calibration silently produces a wrong translation. An exploratory unconstrained-MLP comparison reached high training accuracy while generalizing to only roughly 5.2–6.3% of never-trained combinations. These tests do not isolate a causal effect of architecture, model capacity, or optimization steps.

The system was handed the four-slot compositional grammar. The cultures learned different token assignments; nobody showed that unconstrained agents would invent this grammar unaided.
