    The shortcut’s assumption mattered.

    Detective behaves like Scout until it finds the first wrong sign, then stops inspecting under the assumption that there is at most one error. In the one-error family it gained 42 wins over Scout and lost none, without a rope.

    In the sampled two-error stress set, Detective gained 48 wins but lost 120 relative to Scout. Detecting a second contradiction triggers an explicit stop; that stop itself accounts for some lost wins. The result is not simply “less caution is bad.”

    Strongest scoped conclusion: these fixed policies have different tradeoffs between correction, inspection, recoverability, and action budget. This experiment does not establish how language models behave or how to deploy real agents safely.

    Thirteen prelaunch tests, including a separate brute-force reachability check. Sixty-five post-run assertions passed: all expected records, budget bounds, permissions, raw hashes, no wins in impossible worlds, and no second-error stops inside the one-error family.
