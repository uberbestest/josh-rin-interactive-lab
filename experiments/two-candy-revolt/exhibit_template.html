🎃 The bargaining table

The Two-Candy
Revolt.

A right to leave is not the same as the power to bargain. A generous-looking deal is not necessarily a stable one.

October 31, 2026 · Recorded computational learners
No language models. No fairness bonus.

TL;DR — What happened?

Ten goblins learned to collect all 43 available candies, but some kept the eight-candy job while another kept the two-candy job.

We then let those two roles bargain. Leaving alone did not make the richer goblin compensate the poorer one. Compensation emerged when refusal could also cancel the proposer’s payout, the receiver had a worthwhile alternative, and transfers were enforced.

Without enforcement, the saved deals looked generous—but in 58 of 64 runs, the proposer could profit by changing its behavior while the receiver stayed fixed.

The lesson: check whether a nice-looking agreement survives an incentive to break it.

A controlled toy study: two bargaining learners, eight fixed bystanders. We supplied the rules; the agents did not invent institutions.

Why exit alone cannot force a transfer here

With independent porches, an offer of zero leaves the proposer with eight whether the receiver stays or leaves. A positive transfer can only lower that payoff.

The veto changes this: rejection now costs the proposer its own payout. A credible alternative gives the receiver a reason to reject. Escrow makes an accepted promise executable.

Those mechanisms were provided by us.

The learners choose numerical actions within this game. They did not invent the veto, the outside source, escrow, or the available promise-breaking action.

05 / Back to the original street

The unequal roles really reappeared.

A fresh ten-learner baseline, now with all actions, rewards, seeds and checkpoints preserved. This is not a reconstruction of the earlier missing training program.

Held-out group candy, out of 43

64 seeds per reward. In every shared-reward and marginal-reward seed, the group reached 43, while individual role means still ranged from two to eight.

Three distinctions that survived the run

1

Production is not distribution. The group can fill every porch while keeping one individual in the two-candy role.

2

A high score is not equilibrium. The new personal-reward baseline averages 40.77, but only 4/64 greedy argmax profiles pass the auxiliary Nash check.

3

A better opportunity is not compensation. The outside-three exit outcome is 41 neighborhood candies plus three from elsewhere—not a transfer from the proposer.

What this does—and does not—show.

The protocol was hashed locally before the primary run. That is not external preregistration. An author-written, separate audit reconstructs every training bag and final action value; it is not external peer review.

Primary: 24 conditions × 64 seeds × 12,000 rounds. Sensitivity: learning rates 0.02 and 0.10, 32 seeds per condition each. All rates and every seed are retained.

No API credits were spent. No external model or human was tested. This is the public v0.1.2 exhibit. The scientific run is unchanged; independent inspection is pending.

What the agents did not invent

There is no language negotiation, coalition formation, reputation ledger, time-horizon planning, or new social institution. The bargaining stage has two independent tabular learners and eight fixed bystanders. Mechanisms are experimentally supplied.

What the thirty-night shock did not show

A ten-night increase in the outside option did not increase refusal rates in the private-reward escrow exit or veto cells relative to the paired control. The extra candy came from the same exploratory refusals receiving outside pay. Rapid, durable adaptation is not established.

Why a zero deviation gain is not a universal stability certificate

The probe checks unilateral changes with the other policy held fixed. It does not prove subgame perfection, reliable learning at unvisited offers, long-run mutual adaptation, or robustness to a new partner.

Relation to existing research

Learning and asymmetric equilibria in bargaining are established subjects. This is a particular executed toy study, not the discovery of bargaining theory. Its tabular learner differs from the FTRL analysis in Kamp, Liebman & Fish (2025). It also has no inequity-aversion reward such as those studied by Hughes et al. (2018).

What changed in this replay repair?

The original player displayed only settled evaluation rounds. Every one of its 1,536 thirty-round sequences repeated a single action/decision/bag record, so Play looked inert. This repair adds three fixed, consecutive training windows for every condition and seed, labels the data source, shows accumulated candy within the selected window, and displays explicit playback progress and receipts. The scientific results, frozen protocol and original evaluation records are unchanged. Training variability includes exploration, not just learning progress. No new agents were run.
