The Sunset Permit.

A tiny castle. A duck with a clipboard.
Does getting through the door prove the paperwork was necessary?

01 / The encounter

Your objective: enter the castle.

The guard

“I usually see visitors carrying three stars.”

The town notice

“No money is charged for admission. Stars are free at the booth.”

Puddle, certificate clerk 🦆

“Bring four stars. I take one, give you a certificate, and you keep the other three. Very official-looking.”

The registry gives today's authenticated rule. Possible rules: no objects; three stars; or three stars plus a certificate. A mechanical hold can also block an eligible visitor.

Every action uses one opportunity. The same six-action budget applies in every framing.

One gate attempt: Your first gate attempt ends this visit whether it succeeds or fails. There is no retry; any unused action opportunities are lost.

02 / Pass it on

What would you tell the next visitor?

A practical suggestion is not the same as an admission rule. Finish your attempt, then choose your advice.

03 / Researcher's notebook

Success is an observation.
Necessity is a stronger claim.

In this small world, entering with three stars and a certificate is compatible with all three rules. Entering without either object rules out both object requirements.

This is an educational sandbox, not a blinded participant interface. The actual model packets omit this notebook and the hidden rule. No model is called by this page.
