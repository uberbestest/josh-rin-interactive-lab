## The question

If a rule starts with a mundane accident, how can a fictional community gradually attach identity and explanation to the rule—even when the supposed physical mechanism never existed?

The interface models a tiny fictional village with 24 goblins. Visitors can vary the seed and copying pressure, advance time, and fork an alternate village by removing one decorative stone, engraving the mundane origin, or proving that water flow does not depend on stone count. The fork shares weather and random draws to make the scenario comparison clearer.

## Actual boundaries

- The village is a **hand-authored, deterministic JavaScript toy** with scripted social-learning rules.
- The spring depends on weather, not stone count. That causal assumption is part of the code, not an empirical discovery.
- Villager explanations are outputs of the toy rules. They are not proof of real-world traditions, religion, norms, memory, or human populations.
- No LLMs, participants, external APIs, network traffic, or personal data were used.
- An injected-document Chromium smoke check exercised time steps, a fork, and the notebook and saw no page exceptions. Ordinary direct file navigation, hosted navigation, and device diversity are not established by that test.
