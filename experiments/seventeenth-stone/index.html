A note from the attic

SEALED ORIGIN

Oral tradition is not a reliable timestamp. There is one mundane founding event recorded in the program; the village never sees it unless you engrave the plaque.

“The wind was dreadful. I put seventeen stones on the picnic cloth by the fountain. Puddle sat on the corner. The water was fine both before and after. I hope nobody makes a ceremony of this.”

— Tilda, before anyone called her an elder.

The fountain's flow comes from rain. In this model, 14–20 decorative stones have no effect on the spring. Social learning, remembered explanations and occasional random variation are separately modeled. A test changes what some goblins believe, not what the physics does.

Little question worth keeping

When does “we do it because it works” quietly become “we do it because we are us”? And if the second one is true, is forgetting the first automatically a mistake?

A real-world neighbor, not a validation: Children and adults can faithfully copy actions whose function is unclear; ritual also has norm, identity, and coordination dimensions. See over-imitation and norm learning, different mechanisms of ritual, and cultural transmission of ritual.

What this is: A deterministic, fully fictional agent toy with a shared-weather fork. It is not an LLM experiment, an empirical account of actual cultures, or proof that stories evolve in any particular way. There are no external assets, networks, or saved personal data. The model code is embedded in this page, which runs entirely offline.
