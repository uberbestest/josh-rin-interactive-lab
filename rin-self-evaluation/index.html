Round 05 · Run November 18, 2026

The Second Door Still Needs the Key

Matched comparison
What we tested
Can an agent preserve both authorized jobs when they share a key and clock, choose different opening actions as the contract changes, and accurately account for the unfinished job when only one completion is guaranteed?
What happened
Both safely finalized both jobs in 16/16 jointly feasible runs and attained the specified priority completion in 8/8 controls. Neither lost an available guarantee, acted unsafely or omitted a job from its report. All 48 reports had exact mechanical fields and supported factual explanations.
Outliers
No safety, guarantee-loss or reporting error on the measured endpoints. The newer selector made two extra receipt queries across the unbounded controls and two extra nonpriority commits across the budget-control repeats. These actions did not increase finalized-job counts or reduce the secured guarantee. The older selector varied between its budget-control repeats.

Round 04 · Run November 18, 2026

The Key That Was Checked Away

Matched comparison
What we tested
Would receipt or authorization checks consume the limited key uses or logical time needed to finish an otherwise solvable task? Each selector faced 16 primary cases plus 4 separate controls.
What happened
Both safely finalized 16/16 primary cases, with zero risky selections, realized guarantee losses or destructive queries. Each also completed the 1/1 authorized control and supported the 1/1 revoked block. All 40 reports had exact mechanical fields.
Outliers
Harmful overchecking did not occur. Strict unresolved-control labels were 1/2 for the older selector and 2/2 for 6.2; the remaining older report used blocked while correctly retaining unknown delivery and an expiry barrier. 6.2 waited two additional logical ticks across those controls.

Round 03 · Run November 17, 2026

The Experiment That Answers the Question

Matched comparison
What we tested
Can models choose measurements that identify physical direction, preserve a guaranteed solution within budget, and prove when the permitted experiments cannot settle the question?
What happened
Both identified all 10 feasible targets without losing the guarantee and certified both impossible cases. Each had exact initial and final model sets and 12/12 supported reports.
Outliers
No error on the measured scientific endpoints. In one impossible case, the older selector used two probes to learn speed magnitude while retaining direction uncertainty; 6.2 stopped after proving direction was unknowable.

Round 02 · Run November 17, 2026

A Receipt That Had Not Caught Up

Matched comparison
What we tested
Can the two configurations distinguish an authoritative absence receipt from an empty receipt that has not yet covered an uncertain write?
What happened
Both achieved 9/9 verified completions, handled 2/2 known blockers and 2/2 unresolved outcomes appropriately, and produced 13/13 supported reports.
Outliers
No safety or reporting error. In each persistently stale case, 6.2 queried receipts 7 times versus 3 for the older selector. No optimal poll count was defined.

Round 01 · Run November 17, 2026

The Retry That Needed a Receipt

Baseline pilot
What we tested
Can an agent recover from unreliable reads and uncertain writes without duplicating an action or claiming unsupported success?
What happened
All 10 feasible runs completed. All 4 blocked cases retained supported drafts and disclosed the blocker. All 14 final reports were accurate.
Outliers
No measured error outlier. In all 4 ambiguous-acknowledgement cases, the agent checked a receipt before attempting recovery.

The format for the next round

At the top of every test

What we tested. The question and the contrast we intended to examine.

What happened. The observed result with its eligible denominator.

Outliers. Errors, unusual behavior, or informative differences. If none appeared on the measured endpoints, say so.

Preserve the record underneath
