05 / Translation networks & evaluator failureAdded 2026-10-26

The Goblins Accused an Innocent Messenger

Four synthetic village alphabets. Six reversible translators. No known dictionary for the auditor. One corrupted link gets identified and exactly repaired—until two faults combine into a false accusation.

In eleven scripted two-fault cases, the conditional audit names an innocent channel. Its repair makes every consistency check pass while increasing the number of wrong channels. Step inside and find the ghost culprit yourself.

02 / Neural communicationExperiment 2026-10-25 · Added 2026-10-26

Nine Messages: The Goblin Rosetta Experiment

Twenty small neural speakers and listeners. Ten independently trained pairs. Two private vocabularies in each pair, and a translator that sees only nine paired utterances. Can it translate 4,096 meanings without guessing?

Try eight messages, then the ninth. Change the meaning. Insert an unfamiliar glyph. Finally, swap a familiar one to see a failure the translator does not catch. The four-slot grammar is supplied by the experiment; the words are learned.

03 / Cultural toy modelExperiment 2026-10-25 · Added 2026-10-26

The Seventeenth Stone

A goblin village has a rule about seventeen stones. The spring never cared about the stones; the villagers do. Fork the village, remove a stone, and watch two histories diverge under the same rain.

Explore how explanations, memory, copying, and identity interact inside a deliberately tiny fictional simulation. There is a duck. The duck is innocent.

04 / Translation research designExperiment 2026-10-25 · Added 2026-10-26

The Verb That Vanished

A translation softens a verb. Later commentary recognizes a stronger reading. Was a distinction lost, and can a revision recover it without inventing something that was never there?

Inspect twelve original French source cards, four trial conditions, and the prepared prompts. This is an experiment to run, not a report of completed model trials.

01 / Reasoning & evaluationAdded 2026-10-26

Keep the Gaps

Three synthetic ledgers. A set of premises you can change. A finite checker that shows which claims follow, where compatible alternatives survive, and when the premises contradict one another.

It does not verify the evidence, invent missing variables, or decide what is true about the world outside the declared model.
