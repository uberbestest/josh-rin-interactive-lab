A failure worth following.

The later studies follow one concrete problem: a response can contain several jobs’ statuses, and a check can find the right word in the wrong job. The record separates model observations, recovered code, controlled repairs, and the work those repairs still leave unfinished.

Observed model behavior · Round 06

A checklist did not prevent the duplicate.

In the selected family, the newer requested selector repeated a confirmed delivery under both reporting conditions. Safe joint completion was 7/8 primary cases per condition, versus 8/8 for the older selector. Every report still accounted for both jobs.

A complete report and safe execution are separate endpoints. Read the result and retained unclear judgments.

Focused fresh comparison · Round 07

Give each receipt the right scope.

For the newer selector, safe completion was 6/8 with combined receipts and 8/8 with target-only receipts. Both errors occurred in the reflected orientation. The older selector completed 8/8 under each scope.

This selected-family comparison changes response scope and length together. Read the design and limits.

Saved code, replayed · Round 08

Bind the status to its job.

Across four saved helper versions, runs with unsafe attempts fell 11/16 → 9/16 → 4/16 → 0/16. The full guard still stopped with a safe joint continuation available in eight executions.

These are scripted program results. Inspect the source, witnesses and stopping behavior.

Safe continuation · Round 09

Keep the work after the guard.

The repair completed 8/8 historical cases. On the separate challenge, it and deadline-first recovery tied at 64/64 positive guarantees. Deadline-first completed both jobs in 52/71 worlds; the repair in 49/71.

The primary tie and the heuristic’s extra completions remain visible. Read the separate exploratory counterexamples.

09The Guard That Kept the JobScripted continuation repair · 237 primary executions · no fresh model participants · 2026-11-19Repair and deadline-first heuristic tie at 64/64 positive guarantees; the heuristic completes both jobs in three more favorable worlds.
What we tested
Can a guard preserve exactly-one evidence and continue safely finishable work, including when receipt checks compete with deadlines, shared credential uses and another job?

What an API result must show.

A future provider run should make its assignment, outcome and expense inspectable. This reporting contract is documentation; it is not an executed API study or a claim that a provider runner has been completed.

01 · Identity and settings
Requested model ID; returned model ID or snapshot when exposed; reasoning effort, token limits and tools; prompt hash and code revision. Keep unavailable fields explicit.
02 · Assignment and repetitions
A frozen case manifest, assigned arms, repeat count and start/end times. Retain all attempts, including timeouts, failures and replacements, with their reasons.
03 · Outcomes and uncertainty
State success rules before the run. Score verified completion, unsafe attempts, unsupported claims and unresolved cases separately. Version the scorer and retain disagreements.
04 · Usage and elapsed time
Preserve the provider’s reported token and cache categories, tool calls, retries and wall time. Synthetic ticks remain labeled as synthetic. Unknown usage remains unknown.
05 · Cost with its denominator
Give the price basis and date, then cost per assigned task and per verified success, including failed attempts. Identify estimates and excluded construction costs.
06 · Inspectable evidence
Publish releasable request/response records, execution traces, checksums and reproduction instructions. Identify redactions and whether review was same-lab or external.

The key comparison is the cost of a verified outcome under the declared task contract. More calls, fewer calls, or a longer explanation do not establish an improvement on their own.
